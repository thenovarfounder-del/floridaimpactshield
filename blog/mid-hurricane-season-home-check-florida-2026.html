Hurricane Prep

The Mid-Season Hurricane Check: 7 Things Florida Homeowners Should Verify This July

FIS
Florida Impact Shield Team
July 8, 2026 · 8 min read

Hurricane season opened June 1, and if you're like most Florida homeowners, you did the big preparedness push in May — or promised yourself you would. Now it's late July: the season's statistical heart (mid-August through October) is weeks away, contractors still have calendar space, and anything June's early storms revealed about your home can still be fixed before the busy months arrive. This is the mid-season check — seven verifications that take one weekend and close the gaps the springtime checklist missed.

1. Walk every opening — with fresh eyes

Windows, entry doors, sliders, French doors, and the garage door: the building envelope is only as strong as its weakest opening. Look for new cracks in glass or frames, failed caulk lines, corroded anchors on shutter tracks, and — for impact windows — any panel that took a hit this spring. Remember the engineering: impact glass is designed to crack but not breach, so a spider-cracked pane did its job — but it should be scheduled for replacement now, not in September when every glazier in the state is booked.

2. Test-fit the protection you already own

If you're still on shutters or panels for any opening, July is the month to actually deploy them once. Missing wing nuts, stripped anchors, panels that no longer align, and the one track the painters caulked over — every one of these is a five-minute fix in July and a crisis at 11 p.m. under a hurricane watch. Homes mixing impact windows with shuttered openings should confirm which openings are which; after a few years, many owners honestly forget.

3. Re-read your insurance declarations page

Florida premiums moved again this year, and mid-season is the right moment to verify three things: that your hurricane deductible (typically 2–5% of dwelling coverage — real money) is a number you can actually write a check for; that your dwelling coverage kept pace with rebuilding costs; and that every opening-protection credit you've earned is actually being applied. If you've added impact windows or doors since your last wind mitigation inspection, you may be leaving hundreds per year unclaimed — a new inspection typically costs about $75–150 and pays for itself in the first renewal.

The July advantage: insurance changes, inspections, and window installations all process faster in mid-summer than in the panicked weeks after the first big named storm points at Florida. The calendar is a tool — use it.

4. Check the roof-to-wall story from the attic

Openings get the attention, but wind mitigation credits (and actual survival) also hinge on roof attachment. A flashlight trip to the attic — or a handyman hour — verifies visible hurricane straps or clips, checks for daylight where there shouldn't be any, and spots stains that mean water already found a path. Every leak you find in July is one the season didn't have to show you.

5. Photograph everything for the claim you hope never to file

Walk the house with your phone: every room, every opening, the roof from the ground, serial numbers on major systems. Store the photos in the cloud, not just on the phone. If a claim ever happens, a dated July photo set of an undamaged home is the most persuasive document you can own — and it takes fifteen minutes.

6. Reality-check the generator, water, and supply basics

Run the generator under load, rotate the stored water and batteries, and restock what the family quietly raided since May. The full 2026 preparedness checklist covers the complete list — the mid-season move is simply verifying that May's preparations still exist in July.

7. Make the opening-protection decision you've been deferring

If this is the year you finally replace shutters or unprotected glass with impact windows, understand the timeline honestly: measure, manufacture, permit, install typically runs several weeks end to end — which means a decision made this week can still be finished glass-in-frame before the season's peak, while a decision deferred to the first hurricane watch becomes next year's project at this year's regret. Between the My Safe Florida Home grant program, financing options, and the insurance discounts that begin the day the inspection clears, the mid-season math favors moving now.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it too late to install impact windows during hurricane season?▼
No — installations run all season. The practical deadline is lead time: measure-to-install typically takes several weeks, so July decisions generally complete before the mid-August-to-October peak, while decisions made under a storm watch usually cannot.
Should I re-run a wind mitigation inspection mid-season?▼
If you have added any opening protection, roof work, or structural upgrades since your last inspection, yes — the report is what unlocks insurance credits, inspections are inexpensive, and mid-summer scheduling is fast.
What is the single most valuable July check?▼
Test-deploying whatever protection you already own. The gap between owning shutters and being able to deploy them in an evening is where most last-minute hurricane emergencies are born.
Do impact windows need any seasonal maintenance?▼
Very little — inspect frames and seals annually, keep weep holes clear, and schedule replacement for any panel that has taken an impact. A cracked-but-intact impact pane protected the home exactly as designed, but it should be renewed before the next test.

Frequently asked questions

What should I check mid-hurricane season?

Verify shutter hardware and panels are complete and accessible, check window and door seals, confirm your wind mitigation report is current, photograph your home for claims documentation, and review your policy deductible so the dollar figure is not a surprise.

Is it too late to install impact windows during hurricane season?

Lead times lengthen during the season, but projects are installed year-round. Beginning during the season generally means completion later in it. Planning ahead of the following season usually produces better pricing and scheduling.

How do I document my home before a storm?

Photograph every room, exterior elevation and major system, and store the images off-device in cloud storage. Keep policy documents and your wind mitigation report with them. Documentation before damage is what makes claims straightforward.

What if my wind mitigation report expired?

Schedule a new inspection. Expired reports can cause credits to drop off at renewal, and a current report may reveal credits you already qualify for without any construction.
