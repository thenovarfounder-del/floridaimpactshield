Evacuation Zones vs. Flood Zones: Florida's Two-Map Confusion

Florida Impact Shield · August 7, 2026

Every hurricane season, the same dangerous sentence gets spoken in a million Florida households: “We're not in a flood zone, so we don't need to evacuate.” That sentence mixes up two completely different maps, drawn by two different governments, answering two different questions — and the mix-up gets people hurt. Here's the two-map system, cleared up for good.

Map one: evacuation zones (the leaving map)

Evacuation zones — lettered A through F in most Florida counties — are drawn by county emergency management, and they're about ONE thing: storm surge, the ocean arriving on land. Zone A faces surge first and evacuates first; the letters step inland from there. When officials order “Zones A and B evacuate,” that's this map talking — and it's a question of WATER, not wind: you can sit in a sturdy house that laughs at wind and still be in Zone A because the Gulf can visit your living room. Find yours: search “[your county] evacuation zone lookup” — every county runs an address tool. Check it today, re-check every June; zones get redrawn as surge science sharpens.

Map two: FEMA flood zones (the insurance map)

FEMA's flood maps speak a different alphabet — AE, VE, A, X — and serve a different master: they measure long-term flood RISK for insurance and building purposes. AE and VE are high-risk (the “100-year floodplain,” with VE adding wave action), X is lower mapped risk. This map decides whether lenders REQUIRE flood insurance and what it costs — it says nothing about whether you should leave before a storm. And its most misread letter is X: lower risk is not no risk — roughly a quarter to a third of flood claims come from outside the high-risk zones, because rain-driven flooding never read the map. As we covered in the flood-insurance truth, zone X mostly means flood coverage is CHEAP — which is an argument for carrying it, not skipping it.

The four combinations (all of them exist)

Evacuation zone + flood zone: coastal reality — plan to leave, carry flood coverage. Evacuation zone + zone X: surge risk with cheap flood insurance available — the most underprepared category in Florida, because the X lulls people. No evacuation zone + flood zone AE: inland river and drainage country — you may never be ordered out for surge, but the retention pond has opinions in a wet October. Neither: lucky — but rain still ignores maps, and cheap X-zone flood coverage still exists for a reason. The only way to know your combination: look up BOTH maps for your address — ten minutes, once a year.

Which map answers which question

“Should we leave?” → evacuation map + official orders, full stop — and orders are about surge drowning risk, not whether your walls are strong. “What insurance do we need?” → FEMA map for flood pricing, plus the wind coverage in your homeowners policy — two separate perils, adjusted separately after the storm. “How strong is the storm?” → neither map: that's the category scale, which measures wind while most Florida deaths come from the water the categories don't measure. Three tools, three questions — confusion between them is where preparation fails.

The ten-minute audit (do it this week)

One: county evacuation lookup — write your letter on the fridge. Two: FEMA flood zone at msc.fema.gov — note it beside the letter. Three: if you're in ANY evacuation zone, write the actual plan — where you'd go (a friend inland beats a shelter beats a highway at midnight), what rides along, who's called. Four: price flood coverage regardless of zone — X-zone rates surprise people pleasantly, and the 30-day waiting period means August is already late for this season. Five: with the leaving question settled, harden the staying scenario — the wind-mitigation inspection pays either way. Two maps, one afternoon, zero confusion when it counts.
