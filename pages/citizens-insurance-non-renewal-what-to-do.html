Citizens Non-Renewal or Depopulation: What to Do Next

A notice from Citizens — whether non-renewal, a depopulation takeout offer, or an eligibility change — sets a clock running. Understanding what the notice actually says and what your options are, before the deadline, is the difference between a smooth transition and a coverage gap.

What the notice means

Citizens was created as Florida’s insurer of last resort, and state policy has consistently aimed to move policyholders into the private market when private coverage is available. Depopulation is that mechanism: private carriers offer to take over policies, and policyholders receive notices with a decision window.

A takeout offer is not automatically bad. Private carriers sometimes offer comparable or better coverage, and Citizens policies carry their own limitations. But the offers vary widely, and the deadline to respond is real.

A true non-renewal is different and usually tied to eligibility rules or the condition of the home — commonly roof age or condition, or failure to complete required inspections.

Your options and the clock

Read the notice for the response deadline first, then compare the takeout offer against Citizens coverage line by line: premium, deductibles (especially hurricane), coverage limits, exclusions, and the carrier’s financial strength rating.

Work with an independent agent who can quote multiple carriers rather than accepting or declining in isolation. The takeout offer is one option, not the only one.

Do not let coverage lapse. If you carry a mortgage, a lapse triggers force-placed insurance from your lender, which is typically far more expensive and covers less.

Making your home attractive to carriers

The homes that get offers, and get better pricing, are the ones documenting reduced risk. Roof age and condition dominate underwriting decisions in Florida right now, and opening protection is the largest wind mitigation credit — a home with impact-rated windows and doors on every opening reads very differently to an underwriter than one without.

A current wind mitigation inspection is essential here. Without it, carriers price you on assumptions rather than verified features, and assumptions are never in your favor.

Frequently asked questions

What happens if I ignore a Citizens depopulation notice?

You risk being moved automatically or losing eligibility depending on the notice type. Read the deadline, compare the offer against your current coverage, and respond before the date rather than defaulting.

Is a private takeout offer better than staying with Citizens?

It depends entirely on the offer. Compare premium, hurricane deductible, coverage limits, exclusions and the carrier’s financial rating. Some takeouts are better; some are worse. An independent agent can quote alternatives.

Why did Citizens non-renew my policy?

Common causes include eligibility rule changes, roof age or condition, and required inspections not completed. The notice should state the reason, and roof-related underwriting is the most frequent driver in Florida.

How do I make my home easier to insure in Florida?

Document reduced risk: a current wind mitigation inspection, a roof in good condition and within age limits, and opening protection on every window and door — the largest wind mitigation credit available.
