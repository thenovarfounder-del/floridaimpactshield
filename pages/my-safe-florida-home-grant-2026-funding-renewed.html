Who Qualifies for the Grant

General eligibility requirements include a homesteaded primary residence, a home permitted before 2008, an insured dwelling value at or below program limits, and a completed free wind mitigation inspection. The home must be a single-family residence or eligible townhouse. Qualifying improvements include impact windows, impact doors, roof upgrades, garage door reinforcement, and secondary water barriers.

First-Come, First-Served: Why Speed Matters

Grant funds are awarded on a first-come, first-served basis and deplete quickly during high-demand periods. When funding cycles open, applications surge, and homeowners who wait risk missing out until the next cycle. Checking your eligibility and preparing your application early gives you the best chance of securing the grant while funds remain available.

How to Get Started Today

The first step is a free eligibility check to confirm your home qualifies, followed by the required free wind mitigation inspection that a state-approved inspector performs at no cost. Once approved, you select qualifying improvements like impact windows and coordinate installation with an approved contractor. A free eligibility and savings report can walk you through your specific situation in minutes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the My Safe Florida Home grant still available in 2026?▼
Yes. The program received renewed funding in 2026, making up to $10,000 available to eligible homeowners for hurricane hardening including impact windows. However, funds are first-come, first-served and can deplete during high-demand periods, so checking eligibility and applying early is important.
How much money can I get from the grant?▼
Eligible homeowners can receive up to $10,000 on a 2-to-1 state match, meaning the state contributes two dollars for every one dollar you spend on qualifying improvements. Low-income applicants may qualify for the full $10,000 with no match required.
What are the requirements to qualify?▼
General requirements include a homesteaded primary residence, a home permitted before 2008, an insured value at or below program limits, and a completed free wind mitigation inspection. The home must be a single-family residence or eligible townhouse. Specific criteria can change between funding cycles, so verify current requirements when you apply.
What improvements does the grant cover?▼
The grant covers hurricane-hardening improvements including impact-resistant windows, impact doors, roof upgrades and reroofing to code, garage door reinforcement, and secondary water barrier installation. Impact windows are among the most popular choices because they also unlock significant ongoing insurance savings.
