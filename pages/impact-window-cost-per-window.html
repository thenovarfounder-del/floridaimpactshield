Impact Window Cost Per Window in Florida (2026 Breakdown)

Shopping per-window is how most Florida homeowners actually think about this project — so here are the real 2026 per-window numbers, installed, including the patterns installers do not always volunteer: common vinyl sizes run $400–$900 each; aluminum runs $700–$1,400+; and your per-window price drops as your order grows.

Why Per-Window Price Falls as Count Rises

Permits, mobilization, and setup cost roughly the same whether an installer replaces 3 windows or 15 — so those fixed costs spread thinner across bigger jobs. A 4-window order might price at $850 per window while the identical window in a 14-window order prices at $650. Here is the pattern by project size:

1–5 windows: expect the top of the per-window range ($600–$900 for vinyl standards) because fixed costs dominate. 6–10 windows: the sweet spot begins — $500–$750 typical. 11–15 windows: $450–$700, and installers compete harder for these jobs. 16+ windows: the best per-unit pricing in the market, often $400–$650 for standard sizes.

This is also why replacing your whole home at once usually beats phasing the project over years — you pay the fixed costs once, you reach full opening protection (which is what unlocks the full insurance credit), and you only live through one construction week.

What Is Actually Inside the Per-Window Price

A legitimate installed price includes the manufactured window itself (laminated impact glass in a reinforced frame), removal and disposal of your old window, installation with corrosion-resistant fasteners per the Florida Building Code, waterproofing and sealing, stucco or trim finish, the permit, and the final inspection. If a quote looks dramatically cheaper than the ranges above, check which of those pieces has quietly disappeared — unpermitted installs and skipped waterproofing are where nightmare stories come from.

Getting Your Exact Number

Every home is different — opening sizes, wall construction, county wind zone, and floor height all move the price. The only real number is a written quote measured at your home, and comparing two or three of them routinely saves thousands.
